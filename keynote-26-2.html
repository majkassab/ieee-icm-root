Keynote speaker

Keynote speaker


Noor Fadillah Binti Omar

SMTS Silicon Design Engineer, AMD

Title: When Tuning Knobs Mislead: An Evidence-Driven Approach to Timing Convergence

Abstract: Timing convergence has traditionally relied heavily on engineers’ knowledge, experience, and intuition. Expertise varies across engineers and develops over years of practice, making effective tuning strategies difficult to reproduce, transfer, and scale across design teams. TimingTuneGuide (TTG) introduces an evidence-driven methodology to make timing tuning more systematic and repeatable. Measured design characteristics guide tuning recipes, while controlled experiments and predefined criteria help distinguish repeatable improvements from run-to-run variation. Experiments across dozens of design tiles highlight an important lesson: a tuning idea that works once is not necessarily a rule that should scale. By capturing both successes and failures, TTG helps determine which tuning strategies are worth trusting, reusing, or rejecting. The goal is not to replace engineering expertise, but to turn that expertise into measurable, testable, and transferable knowledge, moving timing convergence from experience-driven trial and error toward evidence-driven engineering decisions.

Biography: Noor Fadillah Binti Omar is a Senior Member of Technical Staff (SMTS) Silicon Design Engineer at AMD in Penang, Malaysia, where she is part of the Front-End Integration team. Her current work focuses on developing evidence-driven methodologies and toolkits for synthesis timing optimization and evaluation across multiple design types.

She brings nearly two decades of silicon design experience spanning digital design, transistor modeling, analog design, physical design, and timing. She began her career at Intel in 2007, designing register files and structured datapath blocks in 32nm and 22nm. She later joined Altera, focusing on transistor modeling and PDK quality assurance (QA) and supporting test-chip development through tape-out in 20nm and 14nm. She then returned to Intel to design and optimize analog I/O circuitry in 10nm and 7nm.

In 2019, she transitioned into physical design, where she owned the end-to-end design of last-level-cache (LLC) TAG blocks, from technology readiness through full signoff. Her work included leading floorplanning, serving as the timing owner for LLC-specific timing, pioneering in-design power analysis enablement across the compute die, and leading the Central Run signoff team across multiple tape-ins.

She holds an MSc in Electronic System Design and a BEng in Electrical and Electronic Engineering, both from Universiti Sains Malaysia.
